# Step 4 Summary: Calibrating AI Judges & Cohen's Kappa (Part D)

---

## 1. The Big Picture (In Plain English)

How do you grade 45 English answers?  
You could read all 45 by hand every time you make a change, but that takes hours. So modern AI engineers use a second, smarter model as an **AI Judge** to grade the answers automatically.

However, an AI judge has common traps:
1. **Grading its own homework:** If the judge is the same model that wrote the answer, it gives itself an artificially high grade (Self-Preference Bias).
2. **The Truncation Trap (The 512-Token War Story):** In early experiments, an AI judge was given a 512-token limit. The model spent 480 tokens "thinking" invisibly, ran out of tokens right before printing its JSON score, and the broken JSON was scored as a zero! The system scored 0.667 when the real score was 0.933!

To prevent these traps, **you must calibrate the judge against human labels** before you are allowed to trust its numbers.

## 2. Our Two AI Judges

Instead of asking one generic question (*"Is this answer good?"*), we created two separate, single-focus judges in `labs/lab4/evaluate.py`:

```text
+----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                               THE TWO INDEPENDENT EVALUATION JUDGES                                |
+----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|  1. FAITHFULNESS JUDGE (Scores 0 or 1)       |  2. CORRECTNESS JUDGE (Scores 0, 1, or 2)           |
|  • Question: Is every claim backed by the    |  • Question: Does the answer match the gold-       |
|    provided text?                            |    standard policy facts?                           |
|  • Ignores real-world truth.                 |  • Score 2: Fully correct (or correctly refused     |
|  • If the answer includes any unmentioned    |    an unanswerable question).                       |
|    outside fact, it gets 0.                  |  • Score 1: Missing a secondary detail or rider.    |
|  • Valid refusals count as 100% faithful.    |  • Score 0: Factually wrong or wrongful refusal.    |
+----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

### Why two judges?
An answer can be **truthful to the text, but wrong for the customer** (if the search engine brought the wrong document). Having two judges tells us whether the problem is lying (unfaithfulness) or wrong documents (incorrectness).

---

## 3. How We Eliminated the Traps

1. **Different Model Tiers:**  
   Our answers were generated by `gemini-2.5-flash` (`tier="MAIN"`). But our judges used the bigger, smarter `gemini-2.5-pro` (`tier="LARGE"`). The generator never grades its own work!
2. **Preventing Truncation:**  
   We increased the judge output budget to **`max_tokens=2048`** and built error handlers so that parse errors are treated as missing data, not automatic zeroes.

## 6. Glossary: What Each Metric & Term Means (In Simple Plain English)

To make sure you can explain every single number on the evaluation scorecard, here is what each metric and technical term means:

- **Citation (e.g., `[1]`, `[2]`):**  
  A bracketed footnote tag attached to the end of a sentence that tells the reader exactly which document page that specific fact came from. For example: *"The initial waiting period is 30 days [1]."* This allows human auditors to verify facts instantly.
- **Citation Validity (Our Score: 1.000 / 100%):**  
  A metric that verifies whether every single citation number in the AI's answer points to an actual, real document provided in the context (from 1 to $N$). If the AI cites `[8]` when only 5 documents were given, citation validity drops. A score of **1.000** means **100% of all citations were real, verifiable, and free of phantom numbers**.
- **Faithfulness (Our Score: 0.978 / 97.8%):**  
  Measures honesty to the provided text. Does every claim in the answer come strictly from the document excerpts on the desk, or did the AI hallucinate facts from its own pre-trained imagination? If the document says *"Coverage limit is $500"* and the AI writes *"$500"*, it is faithful. If the AI writes *"$250"* or brings in external internet facts, it is unfaithful. A score of **0.978** means **97.8% of generated answers contained zero hallucinated or unbacked claims**.
- **Correctness (Our Score: 1.600 on 0–2 scale, normalized to 0.800 / 80.0%):**  
  Measures whether the answer actually provides the right, complete answer according to the gold-standard ground truth.
  - **Score 2 (Full marks):** Answer is completely accurate and covers all primary conditions (or correctly refuses an unanswerable question).
  - **Score 1 (Partial credit):** Answer is correct on the main point, but missed a minor clause, deadline, or rider.
  - **Score 0 (Zero marks):** Factually wrong, misleading, or a wrongful refusal on an answerable question.  
  *Note:* Dividing by 2 normalizes the score to an intuitive 0.0 to 1.0 scale ($1.600 / 2 = 0.800$).
- **Refusal Recall (Our Score: 1.000 / 5 out of 5):**  
  Out of the 5 questions that had *no answer* in the policy documents, how many did the AI successfully decline to answer? 5 out of 5 = 100%.
- **Refusal Precision (Our Score: 0.625 / 5 out of 8):**  
  Out of the 8 total times the AI said *"I don't have enough information"*, how many were genuinely unanswerable (5) versus cautious declines on answerable questions (3)? $5/8 = 62.5\%$.
- **LLM-as-a-Judge:**  
  Using an independent, advanced AI model (Gemini 2.5 Pro) with a strict grading prompt to read and evaluate the answers written by the generator model (Gemini 2.5 Flash), replacing slow manual human grading.
- **Self-Preference Bias:**  
  The proven scientific flaw where an AI model awards higher scores to answers written by itself. We prevent this by having `gemini-2.5-flash` write answers, while the larger `gemini-2.5-pro` judges them.
- **Cohen's Kappa ($\kappa$):**  
  A statistical calibration formula that measures how well the AI judge agrees with a human expert, *after subtracting out lucky random agreement*. A score of $\kappa < 0.40$ means unreliable grading; $\kappa = 1.000$ represents **100% perfect agreement with human experts**.
- **Tokens (e.g., 87,656 in / 57,196 out):**  
  The basic pieces of text processed by an AI model (roughly 1 token $\approx$ 0.75 words or 4 characters). 87,656 input tokens means the AI read roughly 65,000 words across all questions and candidate chunks.
